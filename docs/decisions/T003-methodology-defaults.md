## D05 — Monetary precision, materiality and display

- Parse monetary input as decimal text into `Decimal6` / PostgreSQL `NUMERIC(28,6)`. Reject excess precision, exponent syntax, non-finite values and out-of-range values; never route money through JavaScript `Number` arithmetic.
- Calculate PM, TE and SAD at six decimal places. Do not round PM before computing TE/SAD. Any permitted practical PM adjustment is recorded as an explicit adjusted PM, its unrounded PM, reason, preparer, version and partner approval. The adjusted PM must be within inclusive ±5% of calculated PM; calculate TE and SAD from that approved PM.
- Approved benchmark rate ranges are exactly those in CURRENT: PBT 5–10%, revenue 0.5–2%, total assets 0.5–1%, equity/net assets 1–2%. TE is 50–75% of PM and SAD is 3–5% of PM. Do not silently substitute expenses or a mapped line as a firm-level benchmark. A negative or zero selected benchmark fails closed; no absolute-value conversion or automatic fallback is performed. A reviewer must choose and document a supported positive benchmark.
- Use absolute balance magnitude for threshold classification so a credit balance does not escape a risk band. On unrounded six-decimal values: GREEN iff `abs(balance) < TE`; AMBER iff `TE <= abs(balance) < PM`; RED iff `abs(balance) >= PM`. Significant estimates and high inherent risk force RED regardless of amount. These are routing defaults; they do not replace partner judgment.
- A difference is clearly trivial iff `abs(difference) <= SAD`. Equality at SAD is therefore trivial for accumulation; record the underlying item and rationale, and continue to assess qualitative/significant items independently. Differences above SAD are accumulated and assessed against PM/TE; never net unrelated items to suppress them.
- Present QAR at two decimal places using round-half-to-even, retaining signed values (`-` for negative, no sign for zero). Classification and approval use six-decimal values, not displayed cents. For percentage variance, use `(CY - PY) / abs(PY) × 100`; when PY is zero return `NO_BASE` with a null percentage, not zero or infinity.
- Missing or invalid benchmark, PM, TE or SAD inputs return a validation failure and create no approved assessment. A zero prior-year balance is a valid `NO_BASE` analytical result.

## D06 — Sampling plans and evaluation boundaries

All plans are versioned, bound to an immutable population snapshot and assertion, and require an explicit reviewer-selected sample size or monetary interval and rationale. No row-count heuristic, confidence factor, tolerable-deviation inference or statistical assurance is implied by a method label. Record eligible/excluded IDs and reasons, population digest, stable order, parameters, seed/random start, selected IDs, evidence, evaluation and reviewer disposition.

**MUS (Monetary Unit Sampling):** population consists of positive debit/exposure rows in the selected assertion scope, in frozen population order. Zero rows and negative credit balances are excluded from this monetary population and listed as exclusions; credits receive a separate directed test when relevant, never netted against debits. A positive interval is a required reviewer input. Select the row that crosses each successive interval over cumulative debit exposure, once per row even if a high-value row crosses multiple intervals; a row with exposure at least one interval is consequently certain within this plan. Record every crossing. Evaluate selected items against the assertion, preserve actual detected differences, and do not project or state a confidence conclusion unless a separately approved evaluation model supplies that rule.

**Systematic Random:** snapshot rows are ordered by their immutable sequence and stable ID tie-breaker. Require `1 <= n <= N` and a non-empty eligible population. Generate one unbiased start uniformly in the half-open first interval `[0, N/n)`, store it at the policy's six-decimal sampling precision, and select `floor(start + k × N/n)` for `k = 0..n-1` using decimal/rational arithmetic, de-duplicating only as a mathematically impossible defensive check. `n=N` is a census; empty population, zero/oversized sample and malformed start fail closed. Persist the exact order, start, interval, population digest and IDs so the sample can be replayed. Evaluation reports observed results; projection/confidence requires a separately approved model.

**Stratified Attribute:** define an exhaustive, mutually exclusive set of strata over transaction units for a named binary/multi-valued audit attribute and assertion. Inputs explicitly state the stratum predicate, eligible population, census/key-item rules, per-stratum sample size, seed and rationale. Census/key-item strata are fully tested. Draw the remaining units without replacement inside each stratum using the recorded seed and `STE-SPLITMIX64-DRAW-1` (the versioned 64-bit SplitMix-derived bounded draw used by the current domain); retain the selected IDs in population order. Reject overlapping strata, missing units, duplicate IDs or `n` outside `1..population` (a full-size sample is a census). Report tested count, observed deviations and sample deviation rate per stratum. Do not project deviations, infer confidence or aggregate away a high-risk stratum without a separately approved evaluation model.

The fixture [T003 golden examples](T003-methodology-golden.json) contains hand-calculated boundary, rounding, MUS, systematic and attribute cases. Expected values are independent of the current implementation. They are engineering vectors, not firm-wide ISA assurance tables.

## D07 — Charge-out metric

Preserve the source formula exactly: `charge-out value = sum(logged hours × effective role charge-out rate)` and `source engagement metric = contracted audit fee − charge-out value`. Label it **contracted-fee less charge-out value** (or **charge-out-based margin**), not actual payroll profit, statutory profit or realized cash. Do not substitute payroll, overhead, tax, invoice recognition or partner withdrawals in that metric. For firm expense entry, require explicit account choice: operating categories debit an EXPENSE account and credit an ASSET or LIABILITY account; a partner withdrawal debits an EQUITY or LIABILITY partner-current account and credits an ASSET account. Category labels never select accounts or turn a withdrawal into an expense. An ASSET counterpart records cash settlement at recognition; a LIABILITY counterpart records an unpaid obligation, whose later settlement is a separate journal. Receipt-version attachment and settlement status remain required follow-up behavior; no jurisdiction-specific account mapping is inferred.
